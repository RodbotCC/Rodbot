B. 🏷️ LEAD OWNERSHIP
  OWNERSHIP PRINCIPLES CLARIFICATION


What this Ownership Thing Is:
The organizational structure for Comeketo Catering’s ownership principles emphasizes accountability and responsibility across three roles: Lead Owners, Task Owners, and Opportunity Owners, typically managed by the lead owner. Lead owners are accountable for the lead's success, ensuring timely closure and addressing any issues directly without deflecting blame. Task owners ensure tasks are completed on time and to standard, while opportunity owners see opportunities through to successful completion. Additionally, Account Managers handle B2B account success, and Account Executives guide these managers to align with the company’s goals. Closers, including Event Planners and Catering Coordinators, play a crucial role in converting leads into sales, ensuring booking fees are secured and quotes finalized.
To clarify the heavy and serious responsibility of lead owners, we have established these three ownership principles.
   * Lead Owners
   * Task Owners
   * Opportunity Owners
(usually all 3 of these are the responsibility of the lead owner)
In the sales process:
Lead owners usually own all three roles (Lead, Task, and Opportunity). However, there will be times when a lead owner might need someone else to do some work (task owner) or when the opportunity needs to transition into the operations department for event execution & management (changing the opportunity over to Bibi for execution, for example).
Accountability and Responsibility
As a lead owner, you are the primary person responsible for that lead's success. When approached for accountability, the wrong answer is:
"[Point the Finger at Someone Else] [Because of] - example: This lead has been stuck in the quoting phase for 30 days because Bibi hasn't given us a quote."
Not valid. You are the lead owner. You use any means necessary to ensure the timely and successful closure of your lead's opportunities.
Roles and Responsibilities
💡 1. Lead owners:
   * Are the primary person responsible for that lead's success.
📋 2. Task owners:
   * Guarantee that the work will be done on time, and per our quality.
🚀 3. Opportunity owners:
   * Promise that they will see the opportunity to its successful completion, closure, and cater.
Additional Roles
Account Managers:
   * Are the primary person in charge of an account's success. An account is typically B2B like venues, event planners, and corporate contacts.
Account Executives:
   * Are in charge of the direction and strategy for Account Managers. Ensuring their account success stays in strategic alignment with Comeketo's Goals, Values, and Mission - by ensuring their managers are Visible, Credible, and Profitable throughout the engagement of their accounts.
        Closers: 
   * Comprising both Event Planners and Catering Coordinators, Closers are the pivotal elements of the sales team, responsible for converting qualified leads into successful sales. Event Planners focus on guiding qualified inbound leads through the tasting experience, leveraging their expertise to secure booking fees and close quotes either over the phone or via Zoom. Catering Coordinators, who are highly trained, coached, and mentored by both the Catering Director and Sales Leadership, ensure a booking fee is collected before the potential customer leaves the tasting. They also collaborate closely with other closers to create quotes once the booking fee has been received.

02. 🎙️ CALL CONTROL
In sales conversations, maintaining control is crucial. Using open-ended questions steers the conversation effectively, as "questions steer the conversation, statements kill it." If a yes/no question is necessary, have an open-ended follow-up ready to keep the dialogue flowing. Remember to emphasize techniques like mirroring and tactical empathy, with statements like "It sounds like," to build rapport and trust. By focusing on moving the prospect to the next step and avoiding choice paralysis, salespeople can guide the conversation smoothly and increase the likelihood of closing the deal.


A. 📜 THE THREE RULES


   * Rule #1. Never allow the control of the conversation to deviate from outside of your open ended questions (IF FOR SOME REASON IT DOES GOTO 02. HOW TO MAINTAIN CONTROL)
   * Rule #2. If you have ask a yes/no question always have the next open ended question ready to be asked
   * Rule #3. Be focused on moving your customers to the next step only, gain 1 action, avoid choice paralysis.

B. 🔒 HOW TO MAINTAIN CALL CONTROL
The importance of these sales SDR techniques lies in their ability to enhance communication, build rapport, and gather valuable information so that you may tailor and custom-craft your solutions.

A. Mirror
B. "Sounds Like"
C. Start next open-ended question
A. MIRROR
Why we do this:
Sales mirroring on phone calls, 'Chris Voss', involves repeating the last four words or key phrases your counterpart says to demonstrate active listening and build rapport. This subtle technique encourages the other person to expand on their thoughts, providing more valuable information. By mirroring effectively, salespeople can create a connection, reduce resistance, and guide the conversation toward a favorable outcome.
B. "SOUNDS LIKE" (Tactical Empathy)
Why we do this:
The "Sounds Like" active listening skill involves paraphrasing or summarizing what the other person has said, starting with phrases like "It sounds like," to show understanding and empathy. This technique helps clarify the speaker's message, confirms your comprehension, and encourages them to elaborate further. By using "Sounds Like," salespeople can create a deeper connection with their counterpart, address underlying concerns, and guide the conversation more effectively.
03. "TRANSITION TO NEXT QUESTION"
Why we do this:
Can I make a suggestion?
In sales, using open-ended questions provides significant power and control over the conversation. These questions, which cannot be answered with a simple "yes" or "no," encourage the prospect to share more detailed information about their needs, preferences, and pain points. This deeper insight allows the salesperson to tailor their approach and solutions more precisely. By creating a more engaging and informative dialogue, open-ended questions build trust and rapport, positioning the salesperson as a trusted advisor rather than just a vendor, ultimately increasing the likelihood of closing the deal.

04. 📞 MAIN INBOUND SCRIPT
________________


Goals of this Script
This call script is designed to efficiently convert inbound leads into appointments or tastings by leveraging an intensely curious tone and strategic questioning techniques. It ensures that the salesperson maintains control of the conversation, addresses objections in real time, and deeply engages with prospects to uncover their needs and pain points. This approach is far superior to the old style of selling because it focuses on understanding the customer's vision and concerns, building a genuine connection, and providing tailored solutions, rather than relying on generic sales pitches.


👁️👁️ READ ME FIRST
Before using the script, here's a quick overview
How to Use the Call Script: Step-by-Step Instructions
            1. Initial Contact:
            * Begin the call with an intensely curious tone.
            * Use the appropriate opening line based on the lead source (Facebook, website, expo, general).
            1. Or, if you're feeling bold. Try the experimental "Alternate Opener"
            2. Handle Early Objections:
            * If the prospect says they are busy, use mirroring and "sounds like" techniques.
            * Respond with, "I understand, it sounds like you're really busy at the moment. Can you tell me a little more about what's occupying your time right now?"
            * If they mention a busy schedule, respond with, "Back-to-back meetings, would it be a horrible idea to give me 90 seconds? You can hang up anytime."
            3. Connection Questions:
            * If the prospect doesn’t say "No," proceed with connection questions.
            * Ask, "I’m curious, what inspired you to plan this [event]?" (if unique)
            * or Ask, "What's the vision for your [event]?"
            * Use event-specific questions if they came in on the form to understand their vision.
            4. Situation Questions:
            * Use mirroring and "sounds like" techniques before asking situation questions.
            * Ask about their preferences for food, drinks, and themes.
            5. Pain Points Questions:
            * Determine if they have worked with caterers before.
            * If yes, inquire about their past experiences and why they reached out to you.
            * If no, empathize and reassure them of your experience.
            6. Offer Solutions Questions:
            * Use gathered information to tailor your questions.
            * Ask how providing certain benefits or solving problems would enhance their event.
            7. Highlight Consequences Questions:
            * Address the potential negative impacts of not finding the right caterer.
            * Ask about the effects of unsatisfactory service or food on their event.
            8. Close:
            * Transition smoothly to booking a call or a tasting.
            * Use mirroring and highlight the benefits discussed.
            * Ask, "Would it be a terrible idea to see how we can do that by scheduling a call with our event planner?" or "How about we book a tasting session?"

01. CONVEY CONFUSION 


As to Why They Haven't Accepted a Quote or Don't Want to Move Forward

How to:
                  1. Rising Inflection at the End of Sentences:
                  1. Use a slight rise in pitch at the end of your sentences to convey a question or doubt.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "I'm just wondering, why hasn't the quote been accepted yet?"
                  2. Pausing Before Key Points:
                  1. Pause briefly before mentioning the quote or the decision to emphasize its importance and create a sense of puzzlement.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "I noticed you haven’t... accepted the quote yet. Can you help me understand why?"
                  3. Softened Tone:
                  1. Use a softer, more gentle tone to show that you’re not being confrontational but genuinely curious and confused.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "I'm a bit puzzled as to why you haven't moved forward. Can you share more about that?"
                  4. Expressive Speech:
                  1. Vary your tone to show genuine confusion, such as slightly elongating words or adding a touch of uncertainty in your voice.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "It’s just that I thought everything was clear, but maybe I missed something?"
                  5. Sincere Questioning:
                  1. Use a sincere and earnest tone when asking questions to show that you genuinely want to understand their perspective.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "Can you help me understand what’s holding you back?"
________________


02. SEEM INTENSELY CURIOUS  
Great tones to use in the initial conversation
Remember to feel the vibe and energy of the call, and do your best to:
                  * 01. Talk like a friend
                  * 02. Listen like a psychologist
                  * 03. Ask questions like a teacher
                  * 04. Focus on selecting the proper tone for the context of the call
                  1. Upbeat and Engaging Tone:
                  1. Use an enthusiastic and energetic tone to show genuine interest and curiosity.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "Wow, that sounds fascinating! Can you tell me more about that?"
                  2. Varying Pitch:  
                  1. Use varying pitch to keep the conversation dynamic and show that you are actively engaged and interested.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "I’d love to know, what inspired this idea?"
                  3. Leaning Forward:
                  1. Even though it’s a phone call, act as if you’re physically leaning forward. This can subconsciously come through in your tone, making you sound more engaged.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "What’s the most exciting part of this project for you?"
                  4. Quickening Pace:
                  1. Slightly increase your speaking pace to match your heightened interest and curiosity.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "And then what happened? I’m really curious to know!"
                  5. Mirroring Enthusiasm: 
                  1. Match and mirror the client’s enthusiasm to show that you are equally interested in what they are saying.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "That’s amazing! How did you come up with that?"
________________
03. SEEM CONFIDENT 

In Question Responses
How to
                  1. Steady and Firm Tone:
                  1. Maintain a steady and firm tone to convey confidence in your responses.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "Absolutely, we can handle that for you."
                  2. Controlled Pace:
                  1. Speak at a moderate and controlled pace to show that you are calm and in control.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "Yes, we have extensive experience with that."
                  3. Clear and Articulate Speech:
                  1. Enunciate your words clearly and avoid filler words to project confidence.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "Our team is highly skilled in delivering top-notch service."
                  4. Positive Affirmations:
                  1. Use positive affirmations to reinforce your confidence in your abilities.
                  2. 🗣️ Example: "I’m confident we can exceed your expectations."


________________


04. PACE OF CONVERSATIONS
                  1. Adjusting to the Client’s Speed:
                  * Match the pace of your speech to that of your client. If they speak quickly, pick up your pace slightly; if they speak slowly, slow down to match them.
                  2. Using Pauses Effectively:
                  * Use strategic pauses to give the client time to process information and to emphasize key points.
                  * Example: "This feature will... really enhance your event experience."
                  3. Moderate Pace for Clarity:
                  * Maintain a moderate pace to ensure clarity and understanding, especially when discussing complex topics.
                  * Example: "Let’s go over the details carefully to ensure we’re on the same page."
                  4. Slowing Down for Important Information:
                  * Slow down when discussing crucial information or making important points to ensure the client understands.
                  * Example: "It’s important to note that this service includes... all the additional features you requested."
                  5. Speeding Up for Engagement:
                  * Slightly increase your pace when discussing exciting or engaging topics to convey enthusiasm and keep the client’s interest.
                  * Example: "And the best part is, we can start immediately!"
By effectively using tonality and pacing in your conversations, you can enhance your communication, build rapport, and increase your chances of moving forward with the client.

Use the following template for the check-in message:
Hi [Team Member’s Name],
Just checking in on the status of the quote for [Client Name/Event]. Could you please provide an update on its progress and any anticipated completion time?
                           * Afternoon Follow-Up (Lead Owner):
                           * Follow up again in the afternoon if no response is received by noon.
                           * Use a slightly more urgent tone to emphasize the importance of the task.

2. Internal Communication Protocol


Purpose:


To ensure clear and effective communication between lead owners and internal team members, minimizing misunderstandings and delays.


Steps:
                           * Preferred Communication Channels:
                           * Use designated communication platforms (Close and SLACK) for follow-ups.
                           * Avoid using personal messaging apps to maintain professionalism and traceability.
                           * Clear Assignment:
                           * Clearly assign tasks to specific team members
                           * Use the task management features in Close.com to assign and track tasks.
                           * Document Everything:
                           * Document all communications regarding quote follow-ups in Close.com.
                           * Ensure each follow-up message and response is logged for future reference.
